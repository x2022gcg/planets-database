# overview of planets 
The planets of our solar system orbit the Sun and vary widely in size, composition, and conditions. The inner planets—Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars—are small, rocky worlds located close to the Sun, while the outer planets—Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune—are much larger and mostly made of gas or ice. Each planet has unique features, such as Venus’s extreme heat, Earth’s ability to support life, Jupiter’s massive size, and Saturn’s ring system. Together, the planets show how diverse and dynamic our solar system is.
